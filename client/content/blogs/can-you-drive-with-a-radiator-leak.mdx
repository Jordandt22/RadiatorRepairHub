Short answer: usually no—or at least, not far. It depends on how fast you're losing coolant and whether the engine is already running hot. A tiny seep on a cold morning is different from a puddle growing under the bumper while the gauge climbs.

## When You Should Not Drive

**Pull over and shut it down if you notice:**

- Temperature gauge in the red, or a warning light
- Steam from under the hood
- A strong sweet coolant smell
- Coolant pouring out (a real puddle forming while you sit or drive)
- The engine running rough, losing power, or sounding wrong

Keep going in those conditions and you're gambling with **warped heads, blown gaskets, or a dead engine**. That bill makes a tow look cheap.

## When A Short Trip *Might* Be Okay

A **very slow seep**—a damp spot, not a steady drip—might get you to a nearby shop if:

1. The temperature gauge stays normal the whole way
2. There's enough coolant in the reservoir (checked cold)
3. The drive is short and you can watch the gauge constantly
4. You're not towing, climbing hills, or stuck in stop-and-go heat

Even then, treat it as a one-time exception. Leaks get worse, not better.

## Why Driving On A Leak Is Risky

Less coolant means less heat leaving the engine. Air pockets form. Hot spots show up. And even if you don't overheat today, running low for weeks can wear out the water pump, hoses, and other parts.

## Before You Try To Go Anywhere

1. **Let the engine cool completely** before opening the cap or reservoir.
2. **Check the level** and top off with the right coolant if you can. Plain water is an emergency stopgap only.
3. **Look for the source**—hose, radiator seam, water pump, or cap.
4. **Take the shortest route** to a shop and baby the throttle.
5. **Bring coolant and a tow number.** If the gauge climbs, stop. Don't spend the afternoon topping off on the shoulder.

If you're on the fence, call a tow. And skip chemical stop leak unless a tech specifically recommends it for a limp-home situation.
