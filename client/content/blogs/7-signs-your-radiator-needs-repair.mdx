Radiator trouble doesn't always announce itself with a cloud of steam. More often it starts small—a sweet smell after you park, a heater that suddenly blows cold, a gauge that creeps a little higher than usual. Catch those early and you can skip the tow truck and the engine-repair bill.

Here are seven signs worth taking seriously.

## 1. The Temperature Gauge Keeps Climbing

If the needle drifts toward the red during normal driving, or a warning light comes on, your cooling system isn't keeping up. Pull over when it's safe, shut the engine off, and don't try to "make it home" while it's hot.

Overheating only in traffic often points to the cooling fan. A spike a few minutes after you start driving can be a stuck thermostat. Either way, treat a rising gauge as an emergency, not a curiosity.

## 2. Coolant Puddles Under The Car

Spots near the front of the vehicle often mean a hose, radiator seam, or water pump is leaking. Coolant is usually green, orange, pink, or blue, and it often smells sweet. Even a slow drip deserves attention—small leaks rarely stay small.

## 3. You're Topping Off Coolant All The Time

If the reservoir keeps dropping between fill-ups, something is wrong. Check the level only when the engine is **cold**, and never crack open a hot radiator cap. Persistent low coolant almost always means a leak somewhere, even if you haven't found the puddle yet.

## 4. Steam (Or What Looks Like Smoke) From Under The Hood

Visible steam usually means coolant is boiling or hitting hot metal. Pull over, shut it down, and wait for a full cool-down before you look around. Driving on after that is how warped heads and blown gaskets happen.

## 5. The Coolant Looks Muddy Or Rusty

Healthy coolant should match the color your manufacturer calls for—not brown, oily, or full of grit. Discolored fluid can mean corrosion, contamination, or worse (like a failing head gasket). A shop can flush the system and figure out why it got that way.

## 6. The Heater Blows Cold When It Shouldn't

Low coolant, a clogged radiator, or a stuck thermostat can kill cabin heat even when the engine still "feels" okay at first. If the heater suddenly turns cold after the car is warmed up, have the cooling system checked.

## 7. Gurgling Noises Or A Bulging Hose

Gurgling can mean air trapped in the system. Soft, cracked, or swollen radiator hoses point to age or pressure problems. A hose is cheap insurance compared with overheating on the highway.

## What To Do Next

1. **Don't shrug off a rising gauge or warning light.** Overheating gets expensive fast.
2. **Look for leaks when the engine is cool**—hoses, radiator, cap, and water pump area.
3. **Get a real diagnosis.** A shop that does cooling work can pressure-test the system and tell you what's actually failing.
