When the temp gauge climbs into the red—or worse, steam starts rolling out from under the hood—your cooling system has lost the fight. Keep driving and you can warp a head or blow a gasket. Pull over, shut it down, and figure it out before that happens.

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Pull over safely** and turn the engine off.
2. **Leave the radiator cap alone** while everything's hot. Pressurized coolant can burn you badly.
3. **Wait until it's fully cool** before you check anything. After a hard overheat, that can mean half an hour or more.
4. **Call for a tow** if the gauge won't come down, you see steam, or you're a long way from help.

Nursing a hot car "just a few more miles" is how small problems become engine jobs.

## Common Reasons Cars Overheat

### Low Coolant Or A Leak

Coolant carries heat away from the engine. Lose it through a radiator, hose, or water pump leak and temperatures climb. Look for puddles, a sweet smell, or a reservoir that keeps dropping.

### Stuck Thermostat

The thermostat controls coolant flow. If it sticks closed, fluid can't circulate and the engine can overheat within minutes of driving. A classic clue: the upper radiator hose stays cool while the engine is scorching.

### Clogged Or Damaged Radiator

Rust, sludge, or debris cuts the radiator's ability to shed heat. Road debris can also bend fins or punch holes that lead to leaks and overheating.

### Bad Water Pump

The pump keeps coolant moving. A worn impeller or leaking seal can stop circulation and send temps up in a hurry.

### Head Gasket Trouble

Sometimes coolant disappears *inside* the engine—no puddle on the driveway. White exhaust smoke or milky oil are big red flags.

### Cooling Fan Not Working

Electric fans should kick on at idle or in traffic. When they don't, highway trips can feel fine while stop-and-go cooking starts.

### Old, Neglected Coolant

Fluid that hasn't been changed in years loses its anti-corrosion punch and can clog the system. A flush helps as maintenance—but it won't fix an active leak or a dead fan.

## Signs You Shouldn't Ignore

- Temperature gauge in the red
- Steam from under the hood
- Coolant disappearing with no clear explanation
- Heater blowing cold at idle
- Overheating that comes back right after you top off the reservoir

## What A Shop Will Check

A good tech usually pressure-tests for leaks, checks the thermostat and fan, inspects the water pump and hoses, and looks for head gasket clues if nothing external shows up. Start with someone who handles cooling systems regularly—not every oil-change lane is set up for that work.

## A Few Habits That Help

- Check coolant monthly when the engine is cold
- Follow your manufacturer's flush intervals
- Fix small leaks before they become overheating events
- Watch the gauge in summer traffic and when you're towing
