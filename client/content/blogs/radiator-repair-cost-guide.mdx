Radiator repair bills jump around a lot depending on what failed, what you drive, and where you live. The ranges below are a ballpark so you don't walk into a shop completely blind—always get a written quote for your specific vehicle.

## Common Cooling Repair Costs (U.S. Ballparks)

- **Hose replacement:** $150–$350 (parts and labor)
- **Radiator cap:** $15–$50 for the part; little labor if it's done alone
- **Radiator leak repair (when it's repairable):** $100–$300 — mostly older metal cores; cracked plastic end tanks usually need full replacement
- **Thermostat:** $150–$400
- **Electric cooling fan (fuse/relay):** Often $20–$150 when it's a simple electrical part
- **Fan motor / assembly:** Roughly $250–$600+ depending on the vehicle
- **Water pump:** $300–$750+
- **Radiator replacement:** $400–$900+ (compacts often lower; trucks and luxury models higher)
- **Coolant flush:** Typically $100–$250 for most cars; specialty coolant jobs can hit $250–$400+

## What Pushes The Price Up Or Down

1. **Make and model** — Labor time and parts availability differ a lot between a compact sedan and a heavy-duty truck.
2. **What actually failed** — A hose is cheap. A head gasket is not.
3. **Local labor rates** — Cities and dealers usually cost more than independents.
4. **Parts quality** — OEM vs aftermarket can swing the total by hundreds.
5. **Damage from waiting** — An ignored leak that overheats the engine can turn a few-hundred-dollar job into thousands.

## Repair Vs Replacement (In Plain Terms)

- **Hose or clamp leak:** Fix the hose
- **Cracked plastic tank:** Usually replace the radiator
- **Clogged radiator, no leak:** Try a flush first; replace if flow doesn't improve
- **Keeps overheating after smaller fixes:** Full system diagnosis

## How To Get A Quote You Can Trust

1. Describe symptoms clearly—leaks, steam, low coolant, warning lights, traffic vs highway.
2. Ask what's included: diagnosis fee, parts, labor, coolant, tax.
3. Get it in writing before you approve major work.
4. Compare two or three local shops.

## Ways To Avoid Sticker Shock Later

- Fix small leaks before they cause overheating
- Follow coolant service intervals
- Don't ignore a rising temperature gauge
- Treat stop leak as a temporary band-aid at best, not a permanent fix
