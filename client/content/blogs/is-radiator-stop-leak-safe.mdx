Stop leak sells a comforting story: pour it in, drive away, problem solved. Sometimes it buys you enough time to reach a shop. That's about where the fairy tale ends. It's not a real repair, and using it the wrong way can create a mess that's harder (and more expensive) to clean up.

## What Stop Leak Actually Is

It's an additive that goes into the cooling system. Most formulas use particles, fibers, or chemicals that ride along with the coolant and try to plug small gaps—hoses, radiator seams, a weeping water pump.

Some bottles even claim to fix head gaskets. That's a much bigger promise than sealing a pinhole, and you shouldn't bank on it.

## When It *Might* Help

People reach for it when:

- A tiny external seep shows up and they need to limp to a shop
- They're stranded and just need to get a few miles
- Immediate repair money isn't there yet and they want time

On a **very slow seep** on an older car, it may quiet the drip for a while. Even then, watch the temperature gauge like a hawk—and don't treat the bottle as the finish line.

## The Downsides

Stop leak isn't a long-term plan because it can:

- **Clog the radiator core.** The same particles that plug a leak can block narrow cooling passages.
- **Wear on the water pump.** Some formulas are rough on seals and impellers.
- **Make the real repair harder.** Shops often have to flush everything before they can do proper work.
- **Hide a serious problem.** A head gasket or cracked block won't be fixed by a bottle from the parts aisle.
- **Cause warranty headaches** on some vehicles that discourage aftermarket sealants.

If coolant looks sludgy or the car starts running hotter after you used stop leak, you may need a full flush—and possibly a new radiator anyway.

## The Honest Comparison

- **Stop leak:** Temporary, unpredictable, can clog things, doesn't fix the root cause
- **Real repair:** Finds the leak, replaces the failed part, restores cooling capacity

A $15 bottle looks cheap next to a radiator job. Used wrong, it can turn a simple hose replacement into a system-wide headache.

## What To Do Instead

1. Find the leak (wet spots, sweet smell, dropping reservoir).
2. Don't drive if you're already overheating—stop leak won't cool a car that's in the red.
3. Get a pressure test so you're not guessing.
4. Fix the actual part: hose, cap, radiator, pump, or gasket.

**Bottom line:** Stop leak is occasionally okay as a short-term band-aid to reach a shop. It's not safe to rely on for everyday driving. For head gasket symptoms, repeated coolant loss, or real overheating, skip the bottle and get a diagnosis.
