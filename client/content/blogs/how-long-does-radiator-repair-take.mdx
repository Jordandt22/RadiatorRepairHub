Most cooling repairs are same-day jobs—assuming the diagnosis is clear and the parts are on the shelf. Timing still depends on what failed, how packed the engine bay is, and whether the tech finds more damage once they dig in. Use these ranges to plan a ride or a loaner.

## Typical Timeframes

Once diagnosis is done and parts are in stock:

- **Radiator cap:** Often under 30 minutes if that's the only work
- **Hose or clamp:** About 1–2 hours including cool-down, drain, refill, and bleed
- **Repairable radiator leak:** About 1–2 hours when solder/seal work is realistic (cracked plastic tanks usually mean replacement instead)
- **Thermostat:** About 1–3 hours depending on access
- **Coolant flush:** About 1–2 hours
- **Electric cooling fan:** About 1–3 hours; some assemblies take longer
- **Radiator replacement:** Often 2–4 hours for many cars; trucks and tightly packaged engines can take longer
- **Water pump:** Often 3–6+ hours if belts or timing covers are involved

Overnight stays usually mean parts on order, extra damage found, or a busy shop.

## What Slows Things Down

1. **Parts not in stock** — specialty or OEM radiators may need ordering
2. **Corroded bolts and seized housings** — common on older cars
3. **Deeper diagnosis** — fan electrical checks or head gasket tests add time before repair starts
4. **Multiple failures** — an ignored leak can take out more than one part
5. **Air bleeding** — some systems are picky and need a careful fill so the gauge stays stable after you leave

## Diagnosis Time Vs Repair Time

Expect a separate diagnosis step (and sometimes a diagnostic fee) before any big replacement. A thorough check may include a cold visual, a pressure test, fan and thermostat checks, and a road test after the work.

Ask whether that diagnosis fee gets credited toward the repair.

## Can You Drive While You Wait For An Appointment?

If you're overheating, leaking heavily, or already steamed, don't keep driving—tow it. Mild symptoms with a normal gauge still deserve a prompt appointment; small issues grow into multi-hour jobs.

## How To Make The Visit Smoother

- Describe symptoms clearly (traffic vs highway, steam, puddle color, lights)
- Bring any recent repair notes or coolant type info
- Approve a pressure test early so the right part can be ordered the same day
- Ask for a realistic pickup window—and a call if delays show up

Before you leave, confirm coolant type and level, ask if the system was bled and pressure-checked, get the warranty in writing, and watch the gauge on the drive home.
