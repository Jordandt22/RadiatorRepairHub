When something in the cooling system fails, the question is often: fix what you've got, or put a new radiator in? The right call depends on *where* the leak is, how old the radiator is, and how long you plan to keep the car.

## When Repair Makes Sense

Repair (or a small parts swap) is often smarter when:

- **The leak is in a hose or clamp**, not the radiator core
- **The radiator cap is the weak link** — a new cap can solve pressure loss without touching the radiator
- **There's a small, accessible leak** on an otherwise healthy unit (some shops can still work on certain metal radiators)
- **The radiator is relatively new** and damage is from a one-off hit, not years of corrosion

Cheaper upfront, faster back on the road—as long as the rest of the system is solid.

## When Replacement Is The Better Move

Replace the radiator when:

- **Plastic end tanks are cracked or brittle** (very common; often not worth "repairing")
- **The core is corroded or clogged** and a flush won't bring flow back
- **Leaks keep coming back** after previous fixes
- **The radiator is near the end of its life**
- **Overheating continues** after hoses and the thermostat were ruled out

More money now, fewer return visits later.

## At A Glance

- **Hose or clamp:** Repair
- **Cracked plastic tank:** Replace
- **Corroded or clogged core:** Usually replace (flush first if there's no leak)
- **Minor leak on a newer radiator:** Ask the shop—repair may work
- **Repeated leaks:** Replace

## Cost Ballparks

- **Hose job:** $150–$350
- **Radiator leak repair (when possible):** $100–$300
- **Full radiator replacement:** $400–$900+ depending on the vehicle

Labor, coolant, and related parts add up. Get a written estimate.

## Don't Forget The Rest Of The System

Swapping a radiator without checking hoses, the water pump, thermostat, and coolant condition can leave the next weak link waiting. A solid shop will pressure-test, inspect hoses, refill with the right mix, bleed air, and verify the gauge on a test drive.

## Questions Worth Asking

- Is the radiator itself failing, or is it a hose, cap, or pump?
- Can this unit be repaired reliably, or are you recommending replacement?
- What's included in the quote?
- Should any hoses or the thermostat be done at the same time?

And skip stop leak as a substitute for either path—it can clog the system and create a bigger mess.
