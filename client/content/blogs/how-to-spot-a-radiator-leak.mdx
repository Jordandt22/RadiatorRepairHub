A radiator leak almost never stays polite. What starts as a damp spot under the bumper can turn into an empty reservoir and a steaming hood on a Tuesday commute. The good news: most leaks leave clues before they leave you stranded.

## Warning Signs To Watch For

- **Puddles under the front of the car.** Coolant is often green, orange, pink, or blue, and it usually smells sweet.
- **A rising temperature gauge.** Losing fluid or pressure makes the engine run hotter than it should.
- **Low coolant again and again.** If you're topping off more than occasionally, something's escaping—even if you haven't seen a puddle.
- **Steam or a sweet smell under the hood.** Often coolant hitting hot parts, or boiling from low level/pressure.
- **A heater that suddenly quits.** Low coolant can kill cabin heat, especially in cold weather.
- **White exhaust smoke that smells sweet.** That leans more toward an *internal* problem (like a head gasket) than a simple external drip.

## Where Leaks Usually Come From

1. Corrosion or cracks in an older radiator core or plastic end tank
2. A loose, cracked, or hardened hose and clamp
3. A worn radiator cap that no longer holds pressure
4. A water pump seal starting to weep
5. Road debris or age-related tank damage

Tiny seeps sometimes only show up once the system is pressurized—which is why shops rely on a pressure test instead of guessing.

## Cap Vs Hose Vs Radiator Vs Pump

When the engine is cold, these clues help you describe what you're seeing:

| Likely area | What you might notice |
|-------------|------------------------|
| **Cap / reservoir** | Overflow after driving; wetness around the filler neck |
| **Hose / clamp** | Wet spots along upper or lower hoses; soft or cracked rubber |
| **Radiator** | Wet seams or core; coolant on the radiator face; crusty stains |
| **Water pump** | Drips near the weep hole or pump housing |

## What To Do If You Suspect A Leak

1. **Stop driving if the gauge is red or you see steam.** Let the engine cool completely.
2. **Check the coolant level only when it's cold.** Hot caps are a burn hazard.
3. **Look for wet spots** around the radiator, hoses, water pump, and cap area.
4. **Skip the stop-leak bottle as your first move.** It can clog the system and complicate a real repair.
5. **Get a pressure test and a quote** from a shop that does cooling work.
