A puddle under the car gets your attention fast when the fluid is brightly colored and smells sweet. That is usually **coolant (antifreeze)**, which means the cooling system is losing fluid somewhere. Fix it before you overheat and cook the engine.

For a broader look at leak warning signs, see [How to Spot a Radiator Leak](/blogs/how-to-spot-a-radiator-leak).

## How to tell if it is coolant

Coolant is often **green, orange, pink, blue, or yellow** depending on the formula. It usually has a **sweet smell** and a slightly sticky feel. Oil is dark and slick. A/C condensate is clear, odorless, and usually under the passenger side.

If you are unsure, put a white paper towel under the drip and note the color and smell once the engine is cool.

## Common causes of coolant leaks under the car

### Radiator leak

The radiator can crack at plastic tanks, corrode at seams, or take damage from road debris. Leaks often show up **under the front center** of the vehicle.

### Hose failure

Rubber hoses harden and crack with age. A blown hose can dump coolant quickly. Clamps can loosen or rust and seep at the connections.

### Water pump weep hole

Many water pumps have a small drain hole that leaks when the internal seal fails. You may see drips near the front of the engine, often behind the drive belt area.

### Radiator cap or overflow tank

A worn cap may not hold pressure, so coolant gets forced into the overflow reservoir or out past the cap seal. Cracked overflow tanks leak onto the ground too.

### Heater core (inside the cabin)

If you smell coolant **inside** the car or see wet carpet on the passenger floor, the heater core may be leaking, even if you also see fluid outside.

### Head gasket (internal leak)

Not every gasket failure drips onto the pavement. White exhaust smoke or milky oil without an obvious puddle can still mean coolant loss. Read [Radiator Leak vs Head Gasket Leak](/blogs/radiator-leak-vs-head-gasket-leak).

## What to do when you find a leak

1. **Do not drive if the temperature gauge is high** or you lost a lot of fluid quickly.
2. **Let the engine cool completely** before opening the radiator cap or reservoir.
3. **Check the coolant level** in the overflow tank. Only top off with the correct type if you are low and need to reach a shop.
4. **Look for the source:** hoses, radiator, water pump area, and cap.
5. **Schedule a diagnosis** at a [radiator repair specialist](/category/radiator-repair-service) or [radiator shop](/category/radiator-shop).

Before driving even a short distance, read [Can You Drive With a Radiator Leak?](/blogs/can-you-drive-with-a-radiator-leak).

## What not to do

- **Do not ignore a small drip.** It often gets worse.
- **Do not mix coolant types** without knowing what is already in the system.
- **Do not rely on stop leak long-term.** See [Is Radiator Stop Leak Safe?](/blogs/is-radiator-stop-leak-safe).
- **Do not open a hot radiator cap.** Pressurized coolant can cause severe burns.

## How a shop finds the source

Techs usually pressure-test the cooling system, inspect hoses and clamps, check the water pump and radiator, and look for head gasket indicators if no external leak shows up. A proper diagnosis beats guessing and replacing the wrong part.
