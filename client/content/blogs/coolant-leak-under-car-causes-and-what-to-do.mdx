Finding a puddle under your car is annoying. Finding one that's bright green (or pink, or orange) and smells sweet is a different kind of problem—that's usually **coolant**, and it means the cooling system is losing fluid somewhere. Fix it before the engine cooks.

## Is It Actually Coolant?

Coolant is often **green, orange, pink, blue, or yellow**, depending on the formula. It usually smells **sweet** and feels a little sticky. Engine oil is dark and slick. A/C condensate is clear, odorless water—and it often drips under the passenger side on a hot day.

Not sure? Slide a white paper towel under the drip once the car is cool and note the color and smell.

## Common Causes

### Radiator Leak

Plastic tanks crack, seams corrode, or road debris punches a hole. These leaks often show up under the **front center** of the car.

### Hose Failure

Rubber hoses get hard and brittle with age. A hose can seep for weeks—or burst and dump coolant in seconds. Clamps rust and loosen too.

### Water Pump Weep Hole

Many pumps have a small drain hole that drips when the internal seal fails. Look near the front of the engine, often around the belt area.

### Cap Or Overflow Tank

A weak cap can't hold pressure, so coolant gets shoved into the overflow—or out onto the ground. Cracked overflow bottles leak the same way.

### Heater Core

If you smell coolant *inside* the cabin or notice a wet passenger floor mat, the heater core may be leaking even when you also see fluid outside.

### Head Gasket (Not Always A Puddle)

Some coolant loss never hits the driveway. White exhaust smoke or milky oil with no puddle still means coolant is going somewhere it shouldn't.

## What To Do When You Find A Leak

1. **Don't drive if the gauge is high** or you just lost a lot of fluid.
2. **Let the engine cool completely** before opening anything.
3. **Check the overflow tank** and top off with the correct coolant only if you need to limp to a shop.
4. **Look for the wet spot**—hoses, radiator, pump area, cap.
5. **Book a diagnosis.** Guessing which part failed gets expensive.

## What Not To Do

- Ignore a "tiny" drip (it grows)
- Mix random coolant types without knowing what's already in the system
- Treat stop leak like a permanent repair
- Open a hot radiator cap

A shop will usually pressure-test the system, inspect the obvious leak points, and dig deeper if nothing external shows up. That's faster—and cheaper—than replacing parts at random.
