Not every shop that changes oil is the right place for cooling system work. Radiators, thermostats, water pumps, and pressure testing need the right tools—and recent practice. A good shop gives you a clear diagnosis, fair pricing, and a repair that still holds up a month later.

## Look For Real Cooling Experience

Ask whether they regularly handle:

- Radiator repair and replacement
- Pressure testing and leak detection
- Thermostats, water pumps, and hoses
- Coolant flushes with the correct fluid type

A dedicated radiator shop often sees these jobs daily. Plenty of general auto shops do solid cooling work too. The point is confirming they have the equipment and recent experience with *your* kind of problem—not that they "can order a radiator if they need to."

## Read Recent Reviews With Cooling In Mind

Look for mentions of:

- Honest diagnosis (not selling a radiator when a hose would fix it)
- Clear talk about price and timing
- Repairs that held up after the customer left

## Ask For Diagnosis Before Big Approvals

A good shop will usually pressure-test the system, inspect the obvious parts, explain findings in plain English, and offer repair vs replacement options when both make sense. Vague "your radiator's bad, trust us" without a test is a yellow flag.

## Parts, Warranty, And Coolant Type

- Ask OEM vs aftermarket quality on radiators and major parts
- Get parts and labor warranties in writing
- Make sure they'll use the coolant your manufacturer specifies—especially on newer cars

If someone pushes stop leak as a permanent fix, be skeptical.

## Get A Written Estimate

Before you authorize major work, ask for a line-item estimate that includes:

- Diagnosis fee (and whether it applies to the repair)
- Parts and labor
- Coolant refill and disposal
- Expected time to finish

Comparing two or three shops is normal. You're not being difficult—you're being smart.

## Red Flags

- Pressure to approve a full radiator with no pressure test or clear explanation
- Vague pricing with no breakdown
- Refusal to show you the old parts after replacement
- Guaranteeing a fix without diagnosing first

## Questions To Ask Before You Leave The Car

1. What's your diagnostic process for cooling issues?
2. Do you do this work regularly, or is it occasional?
3. How long will the repair take?
4. What warranty do you provide on parts and labor?
5. Will you use the coolant specified for my vehicle?
