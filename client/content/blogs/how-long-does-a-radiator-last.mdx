Most radiators last roughly **8 to 10 years**, or about **100,000 to 150,000 miles**—but that's a range, not a warranty. Maintenance, climate, and how hard you drive matter as much as the odometer. Some radiators die early from neglected coolant. Others roll past a decade looking fine.

## Typical Lifespan

- **Aluminum radiators (most modern cars):** Often 8–10 years with regular coolant service
- **Copper/brass (older vehicles):** Can last 10+ years, less common on newer cars
- **Plastic-tank aluminum cores:** Extremely common today—and those plastic end tanks are often what cracks first

A seven-year-old radiator with dirty coolant can fail before a well-maintained older one.

## What Shortens Radiator Life

1. **Neglected coolant** — Old fluid loses corrosion inhibitors; rust and sludge build up
2. **Leaks left alone** — Low coolant leads to overheating, which stresses everything
3. **Road salt and debris** — Winter roads and rock hits are rough on fins and tanks
4. **Overheating events** — Even one bad episode can speed up the next failure
5. **Stop leak products** — Temporary sealants can clog narrow passages

## Signs It's Nearing The End

- Frequent leaks or hoses that keep failing after patches
- Visible corrosion, green crust, or white deposits on tanks and seams
- Overheating in normal driving after you've topped off coolant
- Rust-colored or sludgy fluid
- Bent fins or impact damage to the core

## Repair Or Replace?

- **Repair** can make sense for a hose, clamp, or minor leak on an otherwise healthy radiator
- **Replace** is usually the call when the core is corroded, a tank is cracked, or leaks keep returning

Waiting too long isn't just inconvenient—it can turn into real engine damage.

## How To Help A Radiator Last

1. Use the coolant type your vehicle calls for
2. Flush on the schedule your manufacturer recommends
3. Fix leaks promptly instead of living with top-offs
4. Have hoses and the cap checked during routine service
5. Watch the temperature gauge so small problems don't cascade

If you're topping off often, seeing steam, or overheating, get a pressure test from someone who does cooling work regularly.
