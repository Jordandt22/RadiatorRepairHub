A low coolant bottle shows up after a hot day or a slow leak. Topping it off is a small job, and a spill-free funnel keeps the refill cleaner by giving air a path out while you add fluid. The funnel won't tell you why the level dropped.

These steps are general information, not a repair procedure for your car. If you're unsure, the engine is hot, or you can't tell where the coolant went, take it to a radiator shop.

## What The Funnel Is For

A no-spill coolant funnel plugs into the radiator neck or, on many newer cars, the pressurized overflow tank. You fill it above the neck so the system can burp air without sucking the level back down. That's useful after a hose job, a thermostat, or any time the system was opened.

Skip it if the engine is still hot, if you don't know which coolant the car takes, or if the level keeps falling after a refill. Coolant loss means a leak, a bad cap, or something bigger. Adding fluid only hides it for a while.

## Before You Open Anything

Never open a radiator cap or a pressurized reservoir on a hot engine. Wait until the engine is completely cool. Hot coolant under pressure can cause serious burns.

Check the owner's manual for where the system is filled and which coolant spec it calls for. Some cars fill at the radiator. Others fill only at the tank. Color isn't a reliable way to pick a formula. Match the spec: conventional green, Dex-Cool, an Asian-vehicle formula, or a G05-style HOAT. The [tools and supplies shop](/shop) lists examples of each. Don't mix types to use up an old jug.

## How To Use The Funnel

1. Park on level ground and let the engine cool completely.
2. Confirm the fill point and the coolant spec in the owner's manual.
3. Seat the funnel adapter in the radiator neck or tank so it seals. A loose adapter will leak.
4. Pour in the correct coolant until the funnel holds fluid above the neck.
5. Start the engine with the heater on hot, if the manual says to bleed that way, and let the level settle as air burps out. Add fluid to keep the funnel from running dry.
6. When the bubbles stop and the level holds, shut the engine off, remove the funnel, and install the correct cap.
7. After a short drive and another full cool-down, recheck the level.

Follow the funnel kit's own instructions if they differ. Adapters aren't universal.

## When To Skip DIY

Stop if you see steam, if the temp gauge climbs while you're bleeding air, or if the level drops again within a day or two. A funnel can't fix a cracked tank, a leaking hose, a water pump, or a head gasket.

If you aren't sure which coolant to buy, or the car was already topped off with an unknown fluid, have a shop flush and fill it.
