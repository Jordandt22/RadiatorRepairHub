Both problems steal coolant and can overheat the engine—but they're not the same repair. Mix them up and you might replace a radiator when you needed engine work (or the other way around). Here's how the symptoms usually differ.

## Radiator Leak (External)

Coolant is escaping **outside** the engine—from the radiator, a hose, a clamp, the water pump, or the cap. You'll often see:

- Puddles or wet spots under the front of the car
- A sweet smell near the engine bay
- A dropping reservoir without weird exhaust symptoms

Those jobs are often straightforward when you catch them early: hose, cap, or radiator.

## Head Gasket Leak (Internal)

The head gasket seals the engine block to the cylinder head. When it fails, coolant can sneak **into** combustion chambers or oil passages instead of dripping on the driveway.

Common clues:

- **White smoke from the exhaust** (especially on startup) that smells sweet
- **Milky or frothy oil** on the dipstick or oil cap
- Overheating with **no puddle underneath**
- Coolant vanishing quickly with nothing on the ground
- A shop combustion-leak test that finds exhaust gases in the coolant

Head gasket work costs a lot more and takes far longer than most radiator jobs. Don't keep driving if you suspect it.

## Side-By-Side

- **Radiator leak:** External drip, wet hoses or radiator, usually lower repair cost
- **Head gasket leak:** White smoke, contaminated oil, overheating without a puddle, major engine repair

## How A Shop Tells Them Apart

Techs typically pressure-test for external leaks, run a combustion leak test if an internal failure is suspected, check oil and coolant for cross-contamination, and sometimes do a compression test. That's not weekend driveway guessing—get a real diagnosis before you authorize a big ticket.

## If You're Not Sure Which It Is

1. **Stop driving** if the gauge is high or you see steam.
2. **Check for external leaks** only when the engine is cold.
3. **Note exhaust color and oil condition** so you can tell the tech exactly what you saw.
4. **Don't assume it's "just a hose"** because that sounds cheaper.
