A radiator flush clears out old coolant, rust, and gunk, then puts fresh fluid back in. It's solid routine maintenance for a lot of cars. It's *not* a magic fix if you're already leaking or overheating—book a diagnosis first in those cases, not just a flush.

## What A Flush Usually Includes

1. Draining old coolant from the radiator and engine
2. Running cleaner or distilled water through to knock loose sediment
3. Refilling with the correct coolant mix for your vehicle
4. Bleeding air and checking for leaks

The goal is clean fluid, better heat transfer, and corrosion protection. A flush alone won't fix a cracked radiator or a blown gasket.

## When You Actually Need One

Consider a flush when:

- Your owner's manual says it's due (intervals vary a lot—some extended-life coolants go much longer)
- Coolant looks rusty, cloudy, or contaminated
- You're replacing a major cooling part (radiator, water pump, thermostat)
- A shop finds sludge during an inspection

A flush will **not** fix an active external leak. If you're losing fluid between fill-ups, find the leak first.

## What To Expect At The Shop

Most flushes take **1–2 hours**. A good tech will confirm the right coolant type, glance at hoses and the cap while the system is open, check for leaks after refill, and dispose of the old coolant properly (it's hazardous waste).

Ask whether the quote is a full flush or just a drain-and-fill. Those aren't the same service.

## Typical Cost

Ranges vary by location, vehicle, and coolant type:

- **Basic drain and fill:** $100–$150
- **Full flush with cleaner:** $150–$250
- **Luxury or specialty coolant:** $250–$400+

Independents often beat dealer pricing. Get it in writing before they start.

## Flush Vs Other Cooling Jobs

- **Drain and fill:** Fresh coolant, less deep cleaning
- **Flush:** Cleaner pass plus new fluid
- **Radiator repair/replacement:** Fixes leaks or damaged radiators
- **Thermostat job:** Fixes a stuck open/closed thermostat

If you're overheating, low on coolant, or seeing white exhaust smoke, a flush probably isn't the whole answer.

## Before You Book

1. Use the coolant type your manual specifies—mixing types can cause trouble
2. Fix leaks first so you're not flushing money down the drain
3. Compare a couple of local quotes
4. Ask what's included: labor, coolant, disposal, leak check
